# DECISIONS.md

## 1. Why this approach over the obvious alternative?

The obvious alternative was to create a colorful, high-energy SaaS landing page with multiple animations, fake social proof, and heavy visual effects. I rejected that approach.

Instead, I deliberately stayed close to Cal.com’s real brand language: near-monochrome, strong typography, generous whitespace, and restraint. 

Cal.com’s identity is built on clarity, ownership, and professionalism. A loud, over-designed page would have felt inauthentic. The goal was not to make the most flashy homepage — it was to make one that feels like it could actually belong to Cal.com while still creating a strong first impression.

I prioritized honesty and taste over visual noise.

## 2. One trade-off made under time constraints

I kept the page relatively restrained in motion and visual complexity.

With more time, I would have refined the hero composition further, improved depth and micro-interactions on the product mock, and strengthened the lower sections so the quality remains equally high from top to bottom. I would also have spent more time testing and polishing the mobile experience at 390px.

Given the time limit, I chose clarity and brand consistency over adding more visual layers.

## 3. Where AI tools were used and what I verified

AI tools were used for:
- Initial layout structure and component scaffolding
- Generating clean Tailwind classes and responsive patterns
- Helping refine copy to stay honest and on-brand
- Suggesting animation approaches

What I personally owned and verified:
- Final visual hierarchy and spacing decisions
- The decision to keep the design near-monochrome
- Ensuring no fake testimonials, numbers, or logos were used
- The single purposeful micro-interaction on the booking calendar
- Overall tone and positioning (“Scheduling you own”)
- Final polish passes on the hero and key sections

Every major design decision was reviewed and adjusted by me rather than accepted as-is from AI output.
